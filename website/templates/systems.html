Calendar systems

The same sky, counted in different eras. Lahiri ayanamsha is how we place the nakshatras. It is not itself a calendar. Open today’s regional dates or the month panchang.

Vikram Samvat

The lunisolar era used across north India. The year usually opens on Chaitra Shukla Pratipada (Chaitradi). In Gujarat it opens the day after Diwali, Kartika Shukla Pratipada (Kartikadi). The count sits about 57 years ahead of the Common Era, so 2026 CE is Vikram Samvat 2083. Nepal uses the same count under the name Bikram Sambat, but its new year falls in mid-April.

Shaka Samvat

Shalivahana Shaka is the era of the Indian national calendar. The year starts on Chaitra 1, the day after the spring new moon, and the number is the Common Era year minus 78. 2026 CE is Shaka 1948. Marathi practice marks the same opening as Gudi Padwa.

Gujarati

Gujarati months follow the lunar year, Kartikadi Vikram Samvat. Bestu Varas, the Gujarati new year, is Kartika Shukla Pratipada. See the Gujarati row on the regional calendar.

Tamil

Tamil months are solar. A month begins when the Sun enters a rashi. The civil era in wide use is the Thiruvalluvar year. Puthandu falls on Mesha sankranti. The sunrise rule decides which civil date owns a sankranti that falls late in the day.

Malayalam

The Malayalam calendar is solar and counted in the Kollam era. Chingam is the first month. Vishu is the Mesha sankranti new year. Kerala’s sankranti day-boundary is earlier in the afternoon than the Tamil sunset rule.

Bengali

The Bengali panjika is a solar calendar in the Bangabda era. Pohela Boishakh is the new year. If Mesha sankranti falls before midnight, the next civil date is the first of Boishakh.

Assamese

Assamese months follow the same solar sankrantis, counted in the Bhaskarabda era. Rongali Bihu is the new-year festival at Mesha sankranti.

Odia

The Odia panji is solar, counted in the Odia San era. Pana Sankranti (Maha Vishuva) is the new year, on the civil date of Mesha sankranti.

Telugu

Telugu months in common festival use are Chandramana, lunar months. Ugadi is Chaitra Shukla Pratipada. Solar months still name the Sun’s rashi for sankranti.

Kannada

Kannada festival months follow the same Chandramana year as Telugu. Yugadi is the new year. The regional page prints the Telugu/Kannada lunar month for the day.

Marathi

Marathi almanacs use Shalivahana Shaka. Gudi Padwa is Chaitra Shukla Pratipada, the same lunar new year as Ugadi, with the Maharashtrian gudi raised that morning.

Nepali

Nepal’s Bikram Sambat uses the Vikram count, so the year number matches Vikram Samvat. The Nepali new year is solar and falls in mid-April, not on Chaitra Pratipada. Months follow the Sun’s sankranti, close to the way a solar panjika does.

ISKCON / Gaudiya

Gaudiya Vaishnava practice keeps the lunar tithi calendar and often observes Ekadashi on the following day when Dashami is still touching Arunodaya, about 96 minutes before sunrise. The Ekadashi list follows that Smarta/Vaishnava shift. Appearance days such as Gaura Purnima and Janmashtami are on the festival calendar.
